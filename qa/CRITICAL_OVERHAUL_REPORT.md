# Miniature Earth — critical overhaul acceptance report

Local implementation pass A–I. No push or deployment. This fixes the five reported empty dates and the visible future-loss mismatch; it does **not** claim complete scientific coverage of all eras and regions or native-GPU 60 FPS.

**1. Root causes.** Cambrian Anomalocaris and Cretaceous Pteranodon were gated to single snapshot years. The interval validator ignored regional dated coverage. Neogene and early Pleistocene sloth groups lacked North American coverage. Triassic lacked a playable regional animal. Present-day plants and animals were gated to 2026, so 1892 lost otherwise appropriate residents. The population label counts positive simulated species/group entries, including plants; it was not a count of only player introductions. A second rendering bug retained one animal-shaped fossil after local extinction, making the 2100 world look populated despite zeros.

**2. Restored ecosystems.** All five requested ancient dates now load actual animated/observable animal representatives. The Jurassic also restores existing North American Allosaurus and Brachiosaurus using museum/NPS regional evidence. The Carboniferous preset is 308 Ma, inside a reviewed North American Arthropleura deposit interval; arbitrary dates outside reviewed windows remain explicit gaps. Early Pleistocene uses wooded lowlands, not universal snow. Landscape shapes, channels, marine extent and vegetation differ by era.

Negative calendar input is converted from BCE to astronomical year (+1), explaining the one-year display offset. Millennia-scale evidence is not precise to a year.

Available playable entries equal the listed positive entries in the ancient reconstructions and in 1892/2026. In 2030, 2050 and 2100 the available starting catalogue remains all eleven modern taxa, even when the model predicts local losses.

Habitat/evidence: Cambrian is marine (Anomalocaris species, Vauxia species, broad trilobite proxy). Triassic is terrestrial fern/conifer woodland and Desmatosuchus. Carboniferous uses wetland plant groups and Arthropleura. Jurassic uses woodland and three dinosaur genera. At 84.9 Ma the implemented regional animal is Pteranodon: this is still a sparse flying coastal showcase, not a complete dinosaur community. Neogene/early Pleistocene use a broad ground-sloth ecological group and fossil oak genus proxy, not one sloth or oak species persisting for millions of years. Modern/future scenes combine native continental terrestrial, freshwater and coastal representatives; no claim that every taxon co-occurs at one real locality. The catalogue can contain starting taxa whose local future population is zero, with loss explained in inspection.

**4. Modern continuity.** The eleven current North American resident groups are also supported in the conservative 1892 reconstruction; passenger pigeon additionally appears before its documented extinction. Rewind preserves deterministic founders. No claim is made that their abundance was historically constant: starting counts are display assumptions, not census estimates. Coupled ecological changes begin in the educational future/custom simulations.

**5. Visibility.** Wildlife framing, natural clearings, size-aware species focus, representative follow, overview, selection marker and actual canvas selection are operational. Camera focus places the target above the catalogue. General crowds use instancing and LOD; selecting an animal loads its deform rig. Follow is a representative group view across simulation updates, not a documented individual biography. Desktop images show recognizable deer, bison, birds and prehistoric silhouettes; mobile overview is necessarily smaller, with focus/zoom available.

**6. Cambrian marine demonstration.** Three supported marine groups load. Marine inspection reduces surface opacity, frames wildlife and exposes benthic animals. Trilobites now have a segmented three-lobed exoskeleton, dorsal eyes and antennae instead of the previous fish-like form. Vauxia has branched tubes and openings and stays attached. Its inspection focus was enlarged after visual QA. Original models only; no ROM images/models were copied.

**7. Future visual changes.** Positive population sizes drive representative counts. Zero populations depart and vanish rather than leaving misleading permanent animals. Plant biomass and modeled habitat affect foliage density; water availability affects channels and environmental pressure affects colour. Static foliage shadows refresh with habitat/population changes. These are local educational scenarios, not global species extinction predictions.

**8. Conservation comparison.** Same founders, region and seed produce distinct baseline/protection populations and real instance sets. At 2100 baseline retains grass, oak and beaver; protection additionally retains condor and sea otter. Rewind/replay reproduces the numerical outcomes. Both worlds can be switched at the same camera position. Protection does not save all species. The model is uncalibrated; its numerical decline is a consequence of documented teaching parameters, not a validated regional forecast.

**9. Catalogue and UI.** Context defaults to available organisms in the selected era/region. Other/all modes and search preserve experiments. Added habitat/extant filters, scientific names, expandable group/range/region information and empty-result guidance. Existing category and conservation filtering remain. Immersive mode minimizes secondary UI while retaining camera and timeline controls; direct year entry now also works on mobile. Scientific/statistical panels remain closable.

**10. Integrated models.** New Desmatosuchus spurensis (2,738 triangles, height 0.9) and Vauxia gracilenta (1,374 triangles, height 0.45); rebuilt trilobite proxy (2,736 triangles, height 0.42). Base, LOD and transparent 256px Cycles CPU icons pass contracts. Desmatosuchus and trilobite have normalized deform rigs and nine named actions; Vauxia is correctly sessile. Icons were opened and inspected. The small game scale and colours are artistic choices, not measured fossil colours/dimensions.

**11. Playable catalogue.** 72 integrated organisms, including species, genera and explicitly labeled ecological groups. 75 full models including islands; 72 organism LODs; 58 mobile rigs; 205 GLBs total.

**12. References.** 239 additional taxonomy-only entries remain separate and are not included in the playable count.

**13. Evolution.** Rabbit founder variation, inherited reproduction, mutation, environmental selection, differential survival/reproductive success, coat morphology/fur density and Then/Now inspection remain functional. Browser checks advance thirty ecological steps, verify changed allele frequencies, then rewind and replay to the same result. Other existing morph-supported mammals retain their assets. Historical scrubbing does not morph a living animal into an ancestor. Genetics remains a simplified educational system, not species-specific quantitative evolutionary forecasting.

**14. Performance.** ANGLE (Google, Vulkan 1.3.0 (SwiftShader Device (LLVM 10.0.0) (0x0000C0DE)), SwiftShader driver). Above: 1440×1000, Smooth quality, wildlife framing, sixty sampled animation frames per date. These are software-renderer measurements, not physical GPU FPS. General crowd meshes are merged at distant scales, distant updates capped at 30 Hz, and expensive near skeletons reserved for inspected species. Representative population caps were not reduced. The intermediate 2026 close fixture reduced draw calls from 112 to 24 with the same 40 representatives; framing and run conditions also changed, so this is not a controlled whole-game speedup claim. The retained spatial-steering microbenchmark is separately recorded in qa/steering-performance.json and is not game FPS.

Crowded modern mosaic: 66.7 ms median / 100.0 ms p95, 38 calls, 195,196 triangles. The scene report also records resident geometries/textures and render calls per date; GLTF caches retain loaded assets across scrubs. The production build still warns about a large Three.js bundle. No native 60 FPS claim; the existing hardware probe records unavailable native Chrome access.

**19. Remaining limitations.** Scientific coverage is a curated subset. The 357 era/region audit rows in qa/critical-coverage.json explicitly identify empty or sparse combinations, unknown first appearances and feeding evidence. Ordovician/Silurian/Devonian/Paleogene and earlier Holocene regional coverage still have substantial gaps. The five reported dates are populated, but 84.9-Ma and Neogene communities remain especially sparse. Fossil deposit windows express age uncertainty, not continuous occupation or exact species origin/extinction. Oak woods include coarse/reworked deposits; sloths/trilobites are broad proxies. Generic animation cycles and stylized proportions are not biomechanical reconstructions. Water cutaway is an inspection abstraction. Visual representatives are capped, and procedural vegetation is additional to counted representatives. Physical-device/mobile touch and native-GPU performance were not tested; software performance remains below 60 FPS in dense scenes. No claim of complete global paleobiodiversity or calibrated future prediction.

**20. Recommended next improvements.** Expand reviewed locality-specific Cretaceous and Cenozoic communities (with complete assets and feeding evidence), add empirical ecological calibration where possible, then profile physical Mac/mobile GPUs before further shader/animation tuning. Improve underwater floor detail and species-specific gait/behavior while retaining the current style and evidence boundaries.
